#### WR-729. A round bigger than the wall it rolls on takes the material past the wall's rim instead of ending on the body's outline
- **Severity:** Wrong result (the round takes material beyond the face it leaves: the plate under the rim, and from r 154.72 on the plate below, the whole part)
- **Area:** Blend (fillet executive)
- **Location:** solidcore-native, no fork commit. The cliff overflow (WR-631, #960; the fork's `SmFilletCliffOverflow.cpp` at tangency-labs/smlib@8ba4667, and `SmFilletOverflow.cpp`'s `TrimAtCreases`, WR-695) builds a round whose rail runs past its face's rim on the face continued past the rim, and cuts it from the body.
- **What happens:** a 100 x 100 x 10 plate, a top edge rounded r. The ball rolls on the top face and on the 10 mm wall continued below the plate: its centre is (r, 10 - r), and its arc crosses the bottom face at x = r - sqrt(r² - (r - 10)²). Everything above that arc goes, the plate's whole thickness up to the crossing. At r 90 the plate loses its left 48.77 mm through its whole thickness. At r 154.72 the arc passes the far bottom corner and nothing is left; from r 155 the round is refused, naming 154.7 as the largest radius that fits, which no property of the part sets. The same happens to a disc's rim rounded past its wall (R13 x 2 rounded r 2.5 keeps 957.692 mm³, its bottom rim moved in to radius 12.949).
- **Expected:** the round ends on the body's outline, as SolidWorks builds it (#2541: the r 90 plate, 86 577.03 mm³, the reconstruction below matches to 0.001 mm³). The ball stays tangent to the face it keeps and its section passes through the wall's rim, so only the material above that arc goes. On the plate it touches the top at x_c = sqrt(r² - (r - t)²). Past r = (L² + t²) / 2t = 505, x_c would lie past the top face, and the ball touches the far top edge too; the section is the arc from the bottom edge to the far top edge.
- **Trigger:** a lone constant round whose rail runs past a rim of its face, where the ball on the continued face passes inside the rim by more than the body's tolerance. #1467's box rounded r 10.01 (rig `fillets/5158`) passes 5e-6 inside, within the tolerance, so it is not a trigger.
- **How found:** SolidCraft, reported by @MisterMakerNL against SolidWorks (#2541).
- **Confirmed:** in solidcore, `crates/solidcore/tests/wr729_round_ends_on_outline.rs`, against the closed forms of the section.
- **Rust port:** `Kernel::fixes.fillet_round_ends_on_outline`. Off, the cliff overflow is left exactly as it was. With the flag, it needs `fillet_cliff_overflow`, `fillet_refuse_rails_leaving_faces` and `fillet_exact_round_classes`.
  - **The decision** (`crate::blend::fillet_outline::outline_supports`) is made before the round's trace, in its section, for a lone round. Two configurations qualify: a straight edge between two planes, or a circular edge between a plane square to its axis and a coaxial cylinder. A face's rims are its convex, not tangent edges that run the filleted edge's whole length: parallel lines, or whole coaxial circles (`rims_of_face`). A rim counts when the ball's rail on the continued face runs past it and that ball passes inside it by more than the tolerance. The ball then touches that rim and stays tangent to the other face, if its foot there lies on that face and it cuts neither face. Where no such ball exists, it touches both rims, if the ball through them cuts neither face. Anything else is left to the cliff overflow.
  - **The construction** (`FilletSession::end_round_on_outline`, `blend/fillet_executive/session/outline_rims.rs`): the ball touching a rim rolls on the surface tangent to it along the rim. For a straight rim this is the face's plane turned about the rim. For a circular rim it is the wall's cylinder leaned into the cone through the rim (`Cone::lean_cylinder`). Both keep the face's parameterization on the rim. The solver's offset surface of that face, and its geometries' copies, are replaced by the same offset on that surface. The round is then built as a round as tall as its wall: its rail is the rim, its surface the exact cylinder or torus of its class, and it consumes the wall (and, past the bound, the top face).
  - **Results** on the plate, with the flag:
    - r 20 to 500 give the closed form to 1e-11 relative, with 6 faces. The round is tangent to the top face along x = x_c and passes through the bottom edge.
    - r 570 and 600 give 64 874.472 and 64 127.518 mm³, with 5 faces.
    - The disc gives 965.536758 mm³.
    - Nothing is refused at 154.7: there is no such limit.
  - **Left as before:**
    - #1467's box at r 10.01 (rig 5158).
    - A cylinder's end rim rounded r 10 (rig 5236): the end face has no rim.
    - #2299's round breaking into a hollow: its wall's rim is past the rail.
    - Rounds built together with others, which meet in corners this construction does not make.
