#### WR-743. A local operation reverses a partial cylinder by comparing normals at unrelated positions

- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Offsets and local operations
- **Location:** `SmBrep::LocalOperation`; Rust `topology::brep::local_operation::local_operation`, the normal comparison before reversing a replacement surface.
- **What happens:** the replacement normal is evaluated at its natural supporting domain's midpoint, but the original normal is evaluated at its face domain's midpoint. These can be on opposite sides of one cylinder. Their negative dot product then reverses the correctly oriented replacement. The rebuilt face can occupy the wrong angular region; a solid can instead retain a seam UV curve on the wrong side of its periodic domain.
- **Trigger:** a face on the last quadrant of a full cylinder, radius 10 and height 20, replaced by its exact inward offset at radius 9.9. The input face's midpoint is in that last quadrant, while the full replacement support's midpoint is half a turn around the cylinder. Without the correction the operation returns a valid sheet in the wrong quadrant with an inward normal. This happens both with the natural replacement parametrization and with its U parameter reversed beforehand.
- **How found:** SolidCraft #11582, offsetting all lateral faces of a modeled M20 shaft. For its original lower cylinder face, the face domain is `u=[37.1718766,62.8318531], v=[0,1.0252556]`, but the support spans `u=[0,62.8318531], v=[0,20]`. The old face-midpoint normal and new support-midpoint normal have dot product `-.2687647`; at corresponding positions their dot product is 1. The result's seam uses `u=62.8318531` while both adjacent trim curves end at `u=0`.
- **Confirmed:** in the Rust port by the exact native input and the synthetic quarter-cylinder regression. Not run against C++; no C++ numerical result is claimed.
- **Correction:** the local operation evaluates the original face's reference point, minimizes its distance to the replacement surface using the existing exact global point solver, and compares normals at those corresponding positions. This does not assume that replacement parameters match original parameters. The replacement's natural domain is built when lazy. Solver errors and missing solutions propagate; there is no fallback or tolerance change.
- **Evidence:** `local_operation_matches_normals_on_the_same_cylinder_quadrant`, in the existing offset-surface integration test file, checks both replacement parametrizations. The corrected result retains one face, four edges and four vertices, passes the complete level-2 check, has the expected midpoint and outward normal, and has the quarter-cylinder area `9.9 * pi/2 * 20`.
