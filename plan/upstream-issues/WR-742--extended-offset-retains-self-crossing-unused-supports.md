#### WR-742. Extended offsets retain self-crossing surface continuations outside the final faces

- **Severity:** Wrong result
- **Area:** Offsets and local operations
- **Location:** `SmOffsetExecutive::DoExtendedOffset`; Rust `offset::offset_executive::p2::OffsetExecutive::do_extended_offset`, after piecewise merge, stitching and manifold region selection.
- **What happens:** offset construction extends supporting surfaces to find the new boundaries. The final faces occupy only a small part of those extensions, but the returned supporting surfaces retain the full extended domains. An unused continuation can cross itself, so the strict `SmOffsetSurface::AssertValid` test 2 rejects a geometrically regular trimmed face. Shrinking before the final merge is insufficient: the completed face bounds must determine the retained support.
- **Trigger:** an eight-turn rational ruled sheet with four non-isoparametric trim lines, bounded radially near `[.2,.8]`. Its replacement offset support is first restricted to `[.25,.75]` in the linear radial direction. Exact extension is therefore needed to retain the face, and preserving paired UV curves cannot restore that smaller original support. Both signed offsets, -.1 and +.1, return a sheet but fail the strict support self-intersection check without final trimming. This construction uses native rational control nets and curve-on-surface trims, not a screw sweep or imported fixture.
- **How found:** SolidCraft #11582, then reduced to a synthetic native surface in the existing `issue_2048_check_offset_surface_face` integration test. The original modeled M20 offset has 32 such failures; exact trimming of its returned body's unused supports makes every level-2 check pass without changing flags or validation.
- **Confirmed:** in the Rust implementation by the native regression and an independently decoded operation result. Not run against the C++ reference; no C++ numerical result is claimed.
- **Correction:** the completed extended-offset construction runs the existing exact `ShrinkGeometry` once on the completed extended-offset result, after all topology edits and before returning it. It recalculates bounds from the final trims, restricts the supporting surfaces and edge curves, and preserves entity identities and retained geometry. Errors propagate. The self-intersection checker is unchanged; genuine crossings within the retained support remain subject to it.
- **Evidence:** before the correction, both signed offsets reproduced test 2. The `extended_offset_discards_only_unused_support_continuations` regression checks that the corrected operations keep their face/edge/vertex counts, pass the complete level-2 check, retain the final trim curves’ parameter bounds, and evaluate to the original point plus signed distance times its normal at interior parameters. The regression includes the paired-UV preservation construction from WR-746. Independent native all-lateral offsets of both segmented and continuous M20 threads also failed strict validation before final support trimming was added, confirming that paired UV preservation does not replace final support trimming.
- **Geometric context:** self-intersection is an equality between offset points at distinct parameters, and depends on the domain under consideration; see Patrikalakis, Maekawa and Cho, [Shape Interrogation, §11.3.5](https://web.mit.edu/hyperbook/Patrikalakis-Maekawa-Cho/node228.html). This correction reuses exact domain restriction already implemented by the kernel, not a new intersection algorithm or approximation.
